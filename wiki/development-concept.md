# Development Concept

**Workshop mind map.** This page preserves the developer's ideas and source-informed possibilities. Reaction Puzzle, Cargo Rescue and Outbreak Containment now test a bounded subset: see [Play the Experiments](experiments.md) for actual rules. The remaining ideas are proposals, not implemented behavior or approval by appearance here. [Game Modes to Try](game-modes.md) retains the design questions and future candidates.

## Direction and Boundaries

The game could focus on manipulating a living pool of interacting objects: read a situation, set up a useful reaction, launch, and adapt to what remains. Emergent gameplay is a central aim: players should discover solutions through consistent rules, including combinations the level author did not prescribe.

Keep the music generated by contacts and reactions as a supporting feature. Objectives and feedback must remain understandable with audio muted. The core loop should be satisfying before progression rewards or music are added to the evaluation.

The developer explicitly permits invented rules. Cells, viruses, membranes and nutrients are a possible fictional vocabulary, not a biological simulation or educational claim. Literal fire, gas, earth and water remain another presentation option. Decide the rules that produce good play before forcing them into a theme.

Naming is deferred until the loop takes shape. The developer raised a possible conflict around Ricochet; no name or trademark clearance has been performed. Earlier music-led title suggestions are not the current design brief.

## Bubble Actions

Developer ideas: bubbles bounce off things, trap things, pop, split into smaller bubbles, merge, explode, release gas and scatter shrapnel. These can be native bubble types, spawned objects or effects granted by power-ups.

- **Bounce:** redirect movement, knock objects, bank around an obstruction or carry a chain into another chamber.
- **Trap:** capture a creature, object, gas cloud or other hazard; protect or transport its contents.
- **Pop:** release contents, damage something, activate a reaction, or deliberately spend protection. Popping is not automatically good.
- **Split:** divide a payload or produce smaller objects that fit narrow gaps. More objects may increase danger even if each is weaker.
- **Merge:** combine size, stored effects or contents. A larger result may be stronger but harder to move through a passage.
- **Transport and release:** capture something useful or dangerous, move it, then choose where to open the container.

Open questions include whether the player is itself a bubble, how capture is initiated, what can be contained, and whether splitting and merging are automatic or player-triggered.

## Independent Properties

Proposed organizing principle: separate **what an object contains**, **how it moves**, **what triggers it**, and **what it does when triggered**. Do not assume every combination is useful or must be supported.

- **Contents:** empty, nutrient, creature, enemy, fire, gas, acid, earth, water, fragments or a power-up.
- **Movement:** free-floating, fixed, tethered to an anchor, attached to another object, sliding on a track, or heavy enough to require a strong push.
- **Trigger:** contact, deliberate pop, merge, heat, pressure, timer or nearby reaction.
- **Result:** release, split, fuse, push, damage, obscure, slow, swerve, change a surface or create another object.

A tethered gas bubble and a free-floating gas bubble could share their payload rule. An acid effect might release the tether without requiring a special scripted solution. Distinct appearance and readable labels should communicate properties; color alone is insufficient.

## Elements and Status Effects

These are proposed game rules. Exact pairings, ranges, durations, damage and immunities remain unsettled.

- **Explosive bubble:** a blast can push objects and potentially pop nearby bubbles or break a barrier.
- **Gas bubble:** possible damage, poison, obscured visibility, or drunk movement such as swerving and reduced directional control. Combustible, damaging, obscuring and disorienting are separate properties; one gas need not do all four.
- **Fire plus combustible gas:** an explosion can begin a chain. Not every gas must ignite.
- **Steam bubble:** the developer suggested a steam blast or explosion when merged. The required merge partner and trigger are open. Fire plus water producing steam is an additional design hypothesis.
- **Earth plus water:** mud or murk can hide what is present and severely slow the player. Applying slowing to enemies and loose objects too could make it a tool.
- **Acid bubble:** possible contact damage or lingering damage. Corroding obstacles, weakening membranes or freeing anchors are extensions to investigate.
- **Shrapnel bubble:** fragments can hit distant targets, bounce, or pop other bubbles. Fragments may carry effects or be collectible power-ups; those are alternative meanings to test.
- **Poison and slow:** either or both can affect the player. Damage assumes a health resource; slowdown must have a meaningful consequence in the selected mode.

Visibility effects should conceal a bounded region while leaving its outline and the objective readable. Disorientation needs an obvious onset, limited duration and a recognizable end. Immunity or resistance must be legible before the player relies on a reaction.

## Chain Reactions

One event may cause the next through ordinary rules: blast pushes, contact merges, fragments puncture, contents spread, or heat triggers a compatible payload. Chains should also be interruptible by distance, a wall, a trap or an absorbing object.

Example hypothesis: pop a shrapnel bubble, puncture a gas carrier, release gas near fire, then use the explosion to push steam bubbles into a merge. This is a proposed combination, not implemented behavior or real chemistry.

The player should be able to set up a chain, predict its first links, and understand later surprises. Short readable delays and visible propagation can show causality. A chain can help an objective while threatening cargo or healthy cells, making restraint valuable.

Proposed design safeguards: finite payloads, a finite split budget, spent objects that do not immediately retrigger, and source production that can be stopped. Conserve or deliberately account for contents when splitting and merging. Do not allow an automatic merge/split cycle to generate endless resources or prevent a shot from settling.

Persistent outcomes might include clouds, displaced objects, broken barriers and newly opened routes. A follow-up shot should sometimes solve a different problem from the previous one. Generated music can reflect the chain without controlling its success.

## Environment and Layout

Developer ideas include blocks, walls, reactive objects, fixed or constrained bubbles, mazes and varied walled layouts.

- **Layouts:** open pools, mazes, angled bank-shot surfaces, connected chambers, narrow channels and optional shortcuts.
- **Structures:** solid walls, movable blocks, breakable barriers, gates, bumpers and sticky surfaces.
- **Reactive objects:** an anchored object may explode, emit gas, release a pressure wave or spawn bubbles.
- **Changing routes:** a blast moves a block; acid dissolves a compatible barrier; a small bubble passes a gap that a merged bubble cannot.
- **Containment:** walls may shelter the player, separate incompatible contents or concentrate a cloud. Define explicitly which effects they block.
- **Constraints:** tethered objects swing within reach; fixed objects provide reliable aiming targets; a released anchor changes the layout.

Optional extension: an attachment chain can hold a cluster in place until its support breaks. The resulting release could be useful without clearing every member individually.

## Bubble Sources

The developer proposed sources inside the pool in addition to bubbles arriving from off screen.

Possible forms include vents, geysers, plants, creatures, generators, cracks, reaction zones and a player-placed temporary source. They could produce periodically, after impact, when fed, or while a reaction continues.

Possible interactions: plug a vent with mud, uncover it with a blast, redirect its output, change the emitted contents, or shut down an infected producer. Keeping a hazardous source active might supply material needed elsewhere. Production needs a visible cadence and a bounded population; a source must not silently make a level impossible.

## Enemies

Enemies remain optional. Candidate behaviors are pursuit, guarding, eating useful bubbles, popping nearby bubbles, spitting hazardous bubbles and producing new bubbles.

Our proposed systemic approach is to let enemies be pushed, slowed, obscured, trapped and affected by compatible reactions. Players could evade, lure, distract, displace, contain or defeat them. One readable behavior should be tested before adding a large roster.

Example hypothesis: lure a pursuer into mud, trap it, then bounce the container away from a guarded passage. Another possibility is redirecting a hostile producer's bubbles into a useful reaction. Neither requires combat to be the only solution.

## Cellular Campaign

A possible fictional setting uses membrane sacs, cells, viruses and tissue chambers. The player could be a protective cell, an adaptable organism, or an outside actor directing the system; this remains a major decision.

Proposed rules could include selective membrane passage, nutrients spent on repair or abilities, infected producers changing output, division and fusion, and vulnerable healthy structures. These are invented rules chosen for play. No scientific accuracy constraint is imposed.

Possible missions: deliver nutrients, rescue trapped cells, seal a breach, contain an outbreak, protect a producer, restore a damaged chamber or reach an exit. A campaign could introduce one interaction at a time, then combine familiar rules in new layouts.

Candidate campaign loop: enter a region, understand its problem, manipulate objects and reactions, complete an objective under pressure, then choose an adaptation. A health-based campaign and a limited-shot puzzle campaign are alternatives, not an instruction to stack every resource system.

## Objectives and Loss

The developer requires a clear loss condition for the eventual game. Candidate pressures are **time**, **player or protected-object health**, and **limited moves or shots**. Choose a primary pressure per experiment so its effect can be evaluated.

Objectives under discussion include clearing selected targets, capture and delivery, rescue, protecting a structure, surviving a finite encounter, restoring a region and reaching an exit. Optional score rewards should not obscure the actual win condition.

Time encourages speed; health makes exposure and collateral damage matter; limited shots emphasize planning. Continuous steering versus discrete launches is still open. The first suggested trials preserve the existing drag-and-release input to reduce simultaneous changes; that is an experiment design choice, not a permanent control decision.

## Research-Inspired Branches

The following additions are **our extrapolations** from the verified game descriptions in [Research and Sources](#research-and-sources). They are not claims that those games implement our combinations.

- **Capture versus finish:** trapping can be a reversible intermediate state, followed by transport, release or a deliberate finishing action. Bubble Bobble motivates testing more than one use for a container.
- **Visible currents and transport:** flows could route cargo and hazards; a switch or temporary dam could redirect them. This adds planning without another damage type.
- **Fragile cargo:** some bubbles should be preserved and escorted. Bubble Ghost suggests making successful non-popping an objective.
- **Attachment and cluster release:** a compatible group or structural support could release a larger cluster. Puzzle Bobble motivates testing connectivity alongside direct impacts; our physical tether version is a separate proposal.
- **Mass as a tradeoff:** growing could increase force but reduce access through narrow gaps; propulsion or splitting might spend mass. Osmos motivates a cost with both benefits and drawbacks. Test this separately from health and shot limits.
- **Surfaces as lasting consequences:** foam, mud or residue could change traction, cover or routes. FOAMSTARS supplies a reference for a material serving movement, defense and attack.
- **Reaction tools:** expose a small vocabulary of burn, cool, dissolve, push and contain across multiple objects. Noita motivates consistency and discovery, not a requirement for pixel simulation.
- **Information and fair retry:** show the next source output, retain a reproducible starting layout, and offer a restart. This is our playtest design proposal so players can learn rules and we can distinguish deliberate solutions from luck.
- **Cooperation or competition later:** source games demonstrate these formats, but multiplayer is a separate control and networking project. Do not include it in the first trial scope.

## Research and Sources

Verified on November 12, 2026 using developer, publisher or publisher-supplied storefront descriptions. This was source research, not hands-on playtesting. Descriptions establish advertised mechanics, not measured enjoyment, balance, accessibility or suitability for this game. Links open the external source in a new tab.

### Bubble Bobble and Bubble Bobble 4 Friends

**Observed:** Nintendo's publisher description describes trapping opponents, jumping on bubbles, air currents, and lightning/bomb skills. The mobile publisher describes water, fire and lightning bubbles with different effects.

**Takeaway:** a bubble can be a container, a traversal aid and an effect carrier. Our proposed top-down trapping and currents need their own tests.

Sources: [Bubble Bobble 4 Friends publisher description](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Nintendo-Switch-games/Bubble-Bobble-4-Friends-The-Baron-is-Back--1679668.html) and [Bubble Bobble Classic publisher description](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/bubble-bobble-classic/id1497822213).

### Puzzle Bobble Everybubble

**Observed:** Taito describes clearing groups of at least three matching bubbles, survival against a continuing supply, and versus play that sends bubbles to the other board after large clears or detached groups.

**Takeaway:** grouping and connectivity can create a bigger payoff than an isolated hit; changing the supply rule can produce a distinct mode. A match-three grid is not proposed as a required replacement for our physics pool.

Source: [Taito game modes](https://www.taito.co.jp/en/PBEverybubble/gamemodes).

### Pang Adventures

**Observed:** the publisher-supplied Nintendo description lists Tour, Score Attack and Panic modes, alongside electric, explosive, smoke and lava balls.

**Takeaway:** differentiate hazards by readable behavior and separate a finite objective from an endurance challenge. Splitting was already a developer workshop idea; the cited description alone is not evidence for its exact timing or rules.

Source: [Pang Adventures publisher description](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/pang-adventures-switch/).

### Bubble Ghost Remake

**Observed:** the player moves a bubble with gentle blows and protects it through dangers and puzzles; a speedrun format is also described.

**Takeaway:** escorting something fragile provides a contrasting goal to popping everything. Our launch-based delivery proposal differs from its ghost movement controls.

Source: [Bubble Ghost Remake publisher description](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/bubble-ghost-remake-switch/).

### Osmos

**Observed:** the developer describes absorbing motes to grow and ejecting matter to propel yourself, which also makes you smaller. Attractors, repulsors and intelligent motes provide other pressures.

**Takeaway:** movement and size can share a resource, creating a meaningful tradeoff. This is an adjacent physics/cellular reference, not a conventional bubble-popping game.

Source: [Hemisphere Games on Osmos](https://www.osmos-game.hemispheregames.com/).

### FOAMSTARS

**Observed:** Square Enix describes foam used for fast movement, building defensive terrain or high ground, and attacking opponents.

**Takeaway:** one material can change movement, protection and combat. A small persistent-residue system is a candidate; a 3D multiplayer shooter is not the proposed scope.

Source: [Square Enix product description](https://press.na.square-enix.com/FOAMSTARS).

### Noita

**Observed:** the developer describes simulated materials, burning, melting, freezing, evaporation and spells that affect the environment as well as enemies.

**Takeaway:** consistent interactions across objects can support experimentation. This is an adjacent systemic-design reference, not a bubble game; reproducing its simulation is unnecessary for testing a few object-level rules here.

Source: [Nolla Games on Noita](https://noitagame.com/).

## Decisions Still Open

- Player identity and direct control: launch a body, steer continuously, or manipulate the pool externally?
- Primary objective and loss condition: what must happen, and what runs out?
- Which few interactions are understandable and useful in combination?
- How much randomness belongs in layout, source output and reactions? Prefer inspectable inputs and reproducible trials before hidden randomness.
- Which fiction best communicates the winning loop? Cellular, elemental and abstract presentations remain available.
- Which trial should be implemented first? See the [recommended order](game-modes.md#recommended-order).
